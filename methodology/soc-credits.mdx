Soil organic carbon (SOC) sequestration is the process by which organic matter added to soil is stabilised into long-lived humus rather than being rapidly mineralised and re-emitted as CO₂. Carbonkrishi quantifies this for **farmyard manure (FYM)** and **compost** using established dry-matter, carbon-content, and humification coefficients drawn from agronomic literature, then converts the result to CO₂-equivalents and applies a permanence buffer consistent with carbon registry practice (Verra VM0042, Gold Standard). The final output is a credit estimate in **t CO₂-eq/ha/season** and a monetary valuation under India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS).

## SOC Calculation Parameters

The following coefficients are applied to each organic input to determine how much carbon is durably sequestered per kilogram of material applied. All three coefficients are applied multiplicatively.

| Input | Dry Matter (DM) | Carbon Content (C) | Humification Coefficient (H) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FYM (Farm Yard Manure) | 25% | 25% | 25% |
| Compost | 55% | 25% | 35% |

- **Dry Matter %** — the fraction of the fresh weight that is not water. Compost is significantly drier than fresh manure, giving it a higher effective carbon concentration per kg applied.
- **Carbon Content %** — the fraction of dry matter that is organic carbon. Both materials are assumed at 25% C on a dry-weight basis, consistent with standard values in ICAR INM guidelines.
- **Humification Coefficient %** — the fraction of organic carbon that is converted into stable humus rather than being mineralised. Compost's higher value (35% vs 25%) reflects its more advanced decomposition state at the point of application.
- **C → CO₂ conversion factor: 3.667** — the ratio of the molecular weight of CO₂ (44 g/mol) to carbon (12 g/mol). Applied to convert kg of stabilised carbon to kg of CO₂-equivalent.

## Step-by-Step Calculation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Stabilised carbon (kg C/ha)">
    For each input, multiply the application rate by the three coefficients:

    ```
    stabilised_C_fym     = manure_kg  × 0.25 × 0.25 × 0.25
    stabilised_C_compost = compost_kg × 0.55 × 0.25 × 0.35
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="CO₂ equivalent (t CO₂-eq/ha)">
    Convert kg of stabilised carbon to tonnes of CO₂-equivalent using the C→CO₂ factor:

    ```
    fym_soc_tco2     = (stabilised_C_fym     / 1000) × 3.667
    compost_soc_tco2 = (stabilised_C_compost / 1000) × 3.667
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Total SOC before buffer">
    Sum both contributions:

    ```
    soc_before_buffer_tco2 = fym_soc_tco2 + compost_soc_tco2
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Credits after permanence buffer">
    Apply the buffer deduction. The default buffer is 20%, configurable from 0–100%:

    ```
    keep           = 1 - buffer_pct / 100
    credits_tco2   = soc_before_buffer_tco2 × keep
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buffer withheld">
    The portion held in the permanence buffer pool (not eligible for trading):

    ```
    buffer_withheld_tco2 = soc_before_buffer_tco2 - credits_tco2
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monetary value at CCTS price band">
    Credits are valued at India's indicative CCTS price band of ₹600–₹900 per tonne CO₂-eq:

    ```
    value_low_inr  = credits_tco2 × 600
    value_high_inr = credits_tco2 × 900
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why a Permanence Buffer?

Carbon stored in soil is not unconditionally permanent. If land use changes (e.g. the field is tilled, the crop rotation changes, or the organic inputs stop), a fraction of the humus can be re-mineralised and emitted back to the atmosphere. This **permanence risk** is the main reason carbon registries do not issue 100% of estimated sequestration as tradeable credits.

Registry methodologies handle this by requiring projects to contribute a fraction of estimated credits to a **buffer pool** — a collective insurance reserve. Verra VM0042 and the Gold Standard AWD methodology both specify dynamic buffer requirements based on project risk assessments, typically ranging from **10% to 30%**.

Carbonkrishi uses a default of **20%**, which is conservative but broadly accepted. The `buffer_pct` parameter in the `/api/credits` request accepts any value from 0 to 100, allowing project developers to apply the specific buffer prescribed by their chosen registry.

<Warning>
  Buffer rates below 10% are unlikely to be accepted by accredited carbon registries. Use 0% only for internal modelling and sensitivity analysis — not for credit issuance documentation.
</Warning>

## CCTS Price Band

India's **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)** was established under the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, and is jointly administered by the **Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)** and the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)**. It creates a domestic voluntary-to-compliance carbon market that allows agricultural projects to monetise verified GHG reductions and sequestration.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Scheme | Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) |
| Legal basis | Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 |
| Administered by | MoEFCC / BEE |
| Indicative price band | ₹600–₹900 per tonne CO₂-eq |
| Trading platform | NCDEX (National Commodity & Derivatives Exchange) |
| Validation pathway | Registered aggregators on NCDEX |

Credits generated by smallholder farmers are typically aggregated by a registered project developer before being listed on NCDEX, as individual farm parcels rarely produce enough volume to meet minimum lot sizes independently.

## Example Calculation

Consider a 1-hectare paddy plot receiving **10,000 kg FYM** and **1,500 kg compost** per season, with the default **20% permanence buffer**:

**FYM SOC:**
```
stabilised_C = 10,000 × 0.25 × 0.25 × 0.25 = 156.25 kg C
fym_soc_tco2 = (156.25 / 1000) × 3.667     = 0.5730 t CO₂-eq
```

**Compost SOC:**
```
stabilised_C     = 1,500 × 0.55 × 0.25 × 0.35 = 72.19 kg C
compost_soc_tco2 = (72.19 / 1000) × 3.667      = 0.2647 t CO₂-eq
```

**Combined:**

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| FYM SOC | 0.573 t CO₂-eq |
| Compost SOC | 0.265 t CO₂-eq |
| Total before buffer | 0.838 t CO₂-eq |
| Buffer withheld (20%) | 0.168 t CO₂-eq |
| **Tradeable credits (80%)** | **0.670 t CO₂-eq** |
| Value at ₹600/t | ₹402 |
| Value at ₹900/t | ₹603 |

This translates to approximately **₹402–₹603/ha/season** in credit revenue at the current CCTS indicative price band.

<Tip>
  To increase credit potential, raise FYM to 10–12 t/ha and add 1.5–2 t/ha compost. At 12,000 kg FYM + 2,000 kg compost, total credits (after 20% buffer) exceed **1 t CO₂-eq/ha/season** — a meaningful threshold for aggregator registration and viable participation in the CCTS market. Higher FYM rates also improve soil water-holding capacity, reducing irrigation demand and further lowering the field's overall carbon footprint.
</Tip>
